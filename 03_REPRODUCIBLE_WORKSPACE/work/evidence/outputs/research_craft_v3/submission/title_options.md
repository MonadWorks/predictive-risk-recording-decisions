# Title selection after abstract revision

17 October 2026. These are editorial alternatives for the same completed simulated study, not different research claims. All are below the 15-word working limit taken from the conference guidance.

| Choice | Title | Reason and trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Recommended; now in the working manuscript and submission fields | **Predictive Risk and Recording Decisions for Stimulus-Response Estimation in Partially Observed Neural Networks** | Names the inference target and the recording-design problem. It covers both the decision experiment and the fixed-prediction risk test without promising an improved method. 13 whitespace-delimited words (14 if stimulus-response is split). |
| More specific to the approximation being compared | **Evaluating Prior-Averaged Risk for Neural Recording Design** | Makes the methodological comparison immediately clear. More concise, but gives less prominence to stimulus-response prediction and the independent forecast test. |
| Question-led alternative | **Does Prior Averaging Improve Neural Recording Design?** | States the tested question plainly. The abstract must retain the uncertain answer; this is not a claim that the study has identified general success conditions. |

The earlier title, *Predictive Risk and Recording Decisions in Partially Observed Neural Networks*, was accurate but broad. The user preferred retaining “Decisions” and “Partially Observed”; the current title combines these with the explicit stimulus-response estimation target. The recommended title remains descriptive because the primary intervals do not support a declarative improvement claim.

Avoid a title that promises when or why prior averaging succeeds in general: the present work isolates a decision-versus-risk comparison and documents recording-set agreement, but has not separated all effects of linearisation, greedy selection, finite ensembles and nonlinear fitting. Likewise, a claim of validation in living biohybrid robots would exceed the data.

No title choice guarantees acceptance. The final CMT preview and author approval remain separate steps.
