Predicting a neural network's response to a planned stimulus requires recordings that constrain the relevant dynamics. We study how calibration at stimulation and readout sites supports this prediction when connectivity is known but neuronal gains are uncertain.

In a 100-unit model, allocating five recordings to the stimulation site, three readouts and one additional site reduced response MSE by 96.44% relative to five ordinary recordings. Independent validation on forty networks gave a change of -98.13% (95% CI -99.19% to -93.94%). The intervention response was withheld from calibration. A further forty-network study connected this benefit to narrower feasible response ranges, sensitivity to recording precision and the value of endpoint-gain information.

To examine how these recordings constrain the response, we derived an exact factorization into input, recurrent propagation and readout. Forty new networks showed strong contraction of input and readout error contributions under endpoint recording. With the four endpoint gains known, revealing four recurrent gain combinations reduced MSE by 91.45% (95% CI 24.68%–92.57%) relative to revealing four additional ordinary gains. These information interventions supply true parameter values and test which remaining uncertainty matters for prediction.

Endpoint allocation retained a 92.08% MSE reduction under rank-six augmentation and clear benefits under mild connectivity and initial-state errors. Independent random rank-six and positive-gain conditions remained less certain. More elaborate selection criteria showed no robust additional advantage. In the tested networks, endpoint calibration constrains the stimulus-to-readout response, while recurrent gain combinations retain predictive value after endpoint gains are known.
