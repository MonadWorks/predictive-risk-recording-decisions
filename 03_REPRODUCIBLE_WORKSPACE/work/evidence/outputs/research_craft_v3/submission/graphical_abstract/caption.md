Graphical abstract. Does prior averaging improve response-oriented neural recording design or the risk forecast of a fixed prediction? Both studies use simulated networks with known connectivity and uncertain neuronal physiology. A: 40 independent connection matrices; the prespecified broad-prior, endpoint-excluded, three-recording comparisons contrast ensemble with nominal-point risk designs, separately for scalar and common-vector targets. Broad matched uncertainty here means the broad prior and matching generated physiology, not the separate condition named matched. Effects are differences in actual raw response MSE, displayed in units of q^2 times 10^-5, with 97.5% paired network-cluster bootstrap intervals for the two-comparison family. B: 40 new independent networks; the prespecified comparison keeps the local-scalar recording policy, observed data and point predictions fixed and compares their risk forecasts using S(v,e)=log(max(v,1e-24))+e^2/max(v,1e-24), an MSE-functional consistent score. The 95% interval is for ensemble-minus-nominal score. The point predictions, and hence response MSE, are identical between the two forecasts. A and B have different endpoints, scales and confidence levels. Negative effects favor the ensemble; zero is shown in each panel and lies within all three primary intervals. Nested truth/noise/readout repetitions are averaged within network, not counted as independent n. These computational findings do not establish biological validation or a new design/score method.
