Use case: scientific-educational.
Asset: a new manuscript study-overview illustration, not a graphical abstract and not a data plot.
Create a highly refined, clean scientific schematic for a computational neuroscience paper, suitable as a carefully designed conceptual figure in a serious biomedical or computational journal. White background, landscape approximately 3:2. Make it visually explanatory, not a text-heavy flowchart and not a poster. Thin precise vector-like linework, calm balanced spacing, crisp readable sans-serif labels. Navy-charcoal, muted grey, restrained blue #0072B2 and vermilion #D55E00 accents. No gradients, shadows, glossy 3D, decorative icons, fake heatmaps, statistics, logos or journal branding.

Scientific content that must remain exact:
We study a synthetic recurrent rate network with known connectivity J and uncertain neuronal gains and biases θ. We prospectively select a limited recording set, observe calibration activity ONLY at those selected nodes, fit the SAME Gaussian MAP estimator for every design, then predict the time-integrated response to a separate fixed stimulus minus an unstimulated control. Hidden teacher parameters and test responses are available only to the evaluation, not to the selector. This is entirely simulated; do not show an animal, electrode implant, tissue culture, microscope, clinician, real robot, or biological experiment.
Two distinct questions:
1. Recording decisions: change the selected nodes and assess actual response MSE.
2. Risk forecasts: keep the selected nodes, data and point prediction fixed; compare nominal versus prior-ensemble MSE forecasts.
The study does NOT prove an ensemble advantage, so no upward performance arrows, winners, stars, green checks or claims of improvement.

Composition:
Upper two-thirds: an elegant left-to-right visual story. On the left, a compact abstract recurrent network with small circular nodes and fine sparse directed links; three nodes have dark rings to mean a limited recording set. This network is conceptual, not an empirical connectome. A small arrow marked "Fixed stimulus" points into the network, while a modest output bracket marks "Response q". Make the main label "Synthetic network" and small secondary label "Known J · uncertain θ".
From the dark-ring nodes, route three clean parallel lines to a compact "Calibration data" module represented by a simple stack of short schematic signal strokes (no numeric axes, no actual measurements). Then a compact "Common MAP fit" module, then a final "Predict q" module. All labels short and carefully aligned. Keep these visually connected while leaving generous white space.
Lower third: two adjacent, clearly separated scientific comparison motifs rather than paragraphs. Left label "Recording decisions" with two small node-set thumbnails that differ, leading to the short label "Actual response error". Right label "Risk forecasts" with one shared prediction point branching to an open and a filled small marker, leading to the short label "MSE-forecast score". Beneath this right motif write "Same data · same prediction". Do not draw measured values or statistical graphs.
Add one discreet overall note at the bottom: "Conceptual schematic · simulated study".
Use only these supplied labels or mathematical symbols, with exact spelling. Visual hierarchy must foreground the partially observed network and distinguish these two questions at a glance. All arrows must connect named components correctly. Keep text large enough to read at a 180 mm printed width. Do not include a figure number, caption paragraph or title above the illustration.
