Limited recordings constrain how well a fitted neural network predicts a planned stimulus response. We ask which calibration information supports that prediction, and whether averaging a local predictive-risk criterion over physiological uncertainty improves recording decisions.

In a 100-unit rank-two model with known connectivity and uncertain neuronal gains, allocating five recordings to the stimulation site, three readouts and one additional site reduced response MSE by 96.44% relative to five nonendpoint recordings in 40 networks. An independent 40-network validation gave a change of −98.13% (95% CI −99.19% to −93.94%). All recordings concerned calibration activity; the predicted stimulus response was withheld.

A further 40-network study examined the mechanism using alternative parameter fits and controlled information changes. Joint endpoint recording changed the mean squared width of feasible response ranges by -99.08% (95% CI -99.62% to -96.10%) and response MSE by -96.22% (95% CI -99.63% to -79.45%). Degrading endpoint recording precision broadened response ambiguity. Revealing the four endpoint gains and refitting the others changed response MSE by -98.03% (95% CI -99.26% to -92.77%) relative to revealing four ordinary gains. These results support a task-specific role for endpoint information in constraining the predicted response, while individual parameters can remain uncertain.

The original 12-unit comparisons and independent 100-unit confirmation left the additional benefit of prior averaging uncertain. Two covariance-based recording criteria were also evaluated on 40 disjoint networks with endpoints inaccessible. The internal covariance criterion changed MSE by -22.77% (95% CI -52.62% to -5.70%) relative to nominal selection in a secondary comparison, but this advantage weakened under additional fitting starts. The prespecified comparison did not establish an improvement from the held-out-prior criterion. The contribution is a replicated recording-allocation finding and a controlled account of its information mechanism within the tested model family.
