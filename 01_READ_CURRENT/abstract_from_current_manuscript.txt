Limited recordings constrain how well a fitted neural network predicts a planned stimulus response. We ask which calibration information supports that prediction and whether more elaborate predictive-risk criteria improve recording decisions.

In a 100-unit model with uncertain neuronal gains, allocating five recordings to the stimulation site, three readouts and one additional site reduced response MSE by 96.44% relative to five ordinary recordings. Independent validation on forty networks gave a change of -98.13% (95% CI -99.19% to -93.94%). The intervention response was withheld from calibration. A further forty-network study connected this benefit to narrower feasible response ranges, sensitivity to recording precision and the value of endpoint-gain information.

An exact finite-response factorization separates input, recurrent propagation and readout. Forty new networks showed strong contraction of input and readout error contributions under endpoint recording. Revealing four recurrent gain combinations after the four endpoint gains were known reduced MSE by 91.45% (95% CI 24.68%–92.57%) relative to revealing four additional ordinary gains. This dimension-matched information control supports complementary predictive roles for endpoint and internal information. The static combinations capture much of the local response sensitivity, while larger nonlinear changes also depend on trajectory-specific activation slopes.

Endpoint allocation retained a -92.08% MSE change under rank-six augmentation and clear benefits under mild connectivity and initial-state errors. Independent random rank-six and positive-gain conditions remained less certain. Under restricted recording budgets, endpoint-pair lookahead repeated ensemble-greedy choices in 237/240 settings. Prior averaging and covariance-based selection did not establish a robust additional advantage after fitting sensitivity was considered. The contribution is a replicated allocation result and a controlled account of the parameter information supporting held-out intervention prediction.
