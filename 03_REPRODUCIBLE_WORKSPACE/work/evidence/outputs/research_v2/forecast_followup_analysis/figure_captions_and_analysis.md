# Independent risk-forecast follow-up: figures and estimand

The first study's primary tests remain unchanged. A separate hypothesis was frozen before a new independent set of 40 simulated networks (IDs 6000–6039) was generated. The sole follow-up primary is condition broad, endpoints excluded, M=3, local-scalar recording policy. All other conditions/policies are exploratory. Each network contributes 3 parameter truths × 3 noise draws × 3 readouts. These are nested repeats, not independent samples. All rows and all networks, including outliers and flagged failures, are retained.

For the identical point prediction and the identical chosen recording set in each row, the nominal and ensemble risk columns are scored by S(v,e)=log(max(v,10^-24))+e²/max(v,10^-24). Consequently the two forecasts have exactly the same realized raw MSE; any difference is forecast-score performance, not an improvement in the point prediction or a different recording decision. For v>0 and finite MSE, E S=log(v)+MSE/v and its derivative is (v−MSE)/v², so its unique positive optimum is v=MSE (or the floor when restricting to v≥floor). This is a consistent score for the MSE functional. It neither requires Gaussian errors nor demonstrates complete probability calibration, and is not a new scoring rule. General scoring-rule history: [Gneiting & Raftery, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437), with the [verified author-hosted copy](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf). That general background is separate from the direct MSE-functional calculation above.

Scores and errors are first averaged within each J; the paired score difference is ensemble minus nominal, negative favoring ensemble. Ten thousand paired resamples of whole networks (seed 870232) give a single primary 95% percentile interval. Other intervals are pointwise exploratory 95%. RMSE is sqrt(mean network MSE), with that transform recomputed per resample. The first study's outcomes are not pooled into this follow-up and its primary comparisons are not relabelled.

Risk ratios have separate definitions. `risk_ratio_of_aggregate_means` is mean(raw ensemble risk)/mean(raw nominal risk), with a paired cluster-bootstrap ratio interval. `mean_effective_case_risk_ratio` averages individual floor-adjusted case ratios, and need not equal the ratio of means. `risk_ratio_of_network_means` is provided only for the per-network distribution. A zero raw denominator gives NA rather than a silent replacement. Floor counts distinguish strictly-below-floor clipping from at-or-below-floor effective values. In this run nominal/ensemble below-floor counts are 0/0 of 6480 rows. There are 0 flagged optimizer-failure rows and 0 fallback rows; rows remain nested in J.

`forecast_primary_score_difference`: every primary J difference, zero reference and mean with 95% paired cluster CI. `forecast_all_score_differences`: all six condition/policy strata; y ranges differ and heights must not be compared across panels without numerical axes. `forecast_risk_vs_actual`: descriptive J means, common axes and y=x reference; same realized MSE in both panels. `forecast_ratios_and_distribution`: all finite network ratios plus the ratio of aggregate means, and a complete score-difference ECDF with tails retained. Figures are vector PDF with embedded TrueType fonts and 300dpi PNG; no outliers are suppressed.

`paired_case_scores.csv` contains exact row-level scores and forecasts. `network_metrics.csv` supplies the plotted independent-unit values; `stratum_metrics.csv` gives effects and intervals; `leave_one_network_out.csv` diagnoses influence without dropping networks.
